Workshops

Re-story-ing
the marginal.

Dead wood. Building debris. The vine that ate the fence line. Every landscape has features we've been taught to see as waste, nuisance, or threat — the discarded edges of our communities.

Permaculture, and the Traditional Ecological Knowledge (TEK) it draws from, teaches that edges are where life happens. The goal of every workshop is to make life happen, and we do this by engaging with the "marginal" from a place of hands-on curiosity, imagination, and listening for the stories beneath the ones we've been told.

Why We Teach This Way

Not waste.
Unfinished relationship.

A culture that calls something "marginal" has already decided it doesn't matter. We disagree. The margin is where the most honest work is waiting.

This work is called re-story-fication — the practice of giving new, true stories to the things our culture has written off. Dead wood is habitat for foods and medicines. Kudzu is a fast carbon cycle and a fiber crop nobody's harvesting yet. Construction debris is sculpture looking for a second use.

This isn't really about gleaning materials. It's about practicing a different relationship. Picking up and observing something that we've been taught to walk past, slowing down, and asking what is this, and what does it want to become?

Every class is a small repair. Not of the land alone, but of the gap between us and it. The gap that lets us believe we're separate from the systems that feed us. Enough of these small repairs and that gap starts to close. That's how we get back to a world we can actually thrive in instead of one we manage from a careful distance.
